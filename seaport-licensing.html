The legal basis

Why the creative owns it

In many countries the creator is the first owner of commissioned work unless a written contract says otherwise; Singapore's Copyright Act 2021 is one example. Rules differ by country, so Seaport's agreements state it plainly: ownership stays with the creative. IPOS

Why the agreement is in writing

Exclusive licences generally need to be in writing and signed by the copyright owner; Singapore, where Seaport is headquartered, requires exactly that. Every creative signs one with Seaport, wherever they are based. DLA Piper

Non-exclusive or exclusive

The same trade-off as stock licensing: royalty-free licences are non-exclusive, so others can use the same work; rights-managed licences can be exclusive for a term and territory. Stocksy

Licensing questions

Who owns the work?

The creative who made it. Seaport holds the right to license it, on terms the creative has signed, and never takes ownership.

Can we own the work outright?

Only if the creative agrees directly. For commissioned projects you can negotiate a full transfer with them; otherwise you get a licence for the use you need.

What's the difference between Standard, Extended and Exclusive?

Standard covers one brand and one campaign or product. Extended covers several brands or markets, or paid distribution. Exclusive takes the framework out of the library in your category and territory for a set term. Standard and Extended are non-exclusive.

Can our competitors license the same framework?

With a Standard or Extended licence, yes: they're non-exclusive. An Exclusive licence stops anyone else licensing it in your category and territory for the term you choose.

Who adapts a framework to our brand?

Usually the creative who made it. Adaptation is quoted separately from the licence.

How do creatives get paid?

Seaport collects the licence fee and pays the creative a royalty every time their work is licensed. Commissioned work is paid as a project fee.

Is this legal advice?

No. This page is a summary for illustration. Contract terms should be reviewed by IP counsel in each country Seaport operates in.
