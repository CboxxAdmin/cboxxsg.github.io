The relay, from first rotation to your next job

Fifteen months across five companies, then up to three months of cool-down. Here's what happens at every stage, and the rules that keep it fair.

The five rotations

Why five, and why three months

Three months is long enough to do real work and short enough to try something new. Five hosts give you breadth, depth and five employers who've seen you work.

Month 1Month 15Up to month 18

    For the host company

    Five different companies

    Each rotation is with a new host. No company hosts the same person twice.

    Planned, not random

    Your coach plans the order: familiar, stretch, new field, deeper, audition.

    Leave any time for a job

    A job offer always wins. The relay ends the day you accept.

    Cool-down

    Train, rest and land the job

    After fifteen months of moving, cool-down is time to close gaps and focus on the right job, with a basic allowance so you're not job hunting on nothing.

    • Up to three monthsIt ends as soon as you start a job.
    • Training on your real gapsCourses chosen from what your five reviews said, not a generic list.
    • A minimum allowanceIndicatively S$800 a month while you train and apply.
    • Your hosts get first lookAll five companies see that you're available before anyone else.

    Skills Passport

    Five reviews that belong to you

    At the end of every rotation your host reviews the same six skills plus the skills of the role. The passport shows how you've grown, verified by five employers.

    • You own itShare a link with any employer, inside or outside Seaport.
    • You can respondAdd your side to any review before it's final.
    Safeguards

    Rules that keep it fair

    A programme that moves people between companies needs clear rules. These would be written into every placement agreement.

    Written terms

    An employment contract with Seaport and a placement agreement with every host: hours, duties, leave and pay.

    Insurance and safety

    Work injury insurance throughout, and site safety briefings before every rotation.

    Real work, not free labour

    Hosts pay the full host fee, and participants can't replace existing staff.

    A way to raise concerns

    Participants can report problems to their coach or an independent contact, without affecting their review.

    Your data

    Passports are shared only with hosts and employers you choose, handled under Singapore's PDPA.

    Inclusive by default

    Reasonable adjustments for disability, caring duties and health are planned in at the start.
